# Avatar desk assets

The `/avatar` concept page plays `public/avatar/desk/`: silent videos of one filmed man at a desk, made from a single stock clip, and three pre-rendered spoken answers. `public/avatar/desk/presence.json` lists them. Every video starts and ends on the same rest frame, so any video can follow any other with a 100 ms crossfade between two practically identical frames.

This is stock footage with a synthetic voice. The man is not a team member and never said these words. In the prepared answers the mouth region is generated by a lip-sync model when `presence.json` says `"lipSync": "musetalk"` for that answer; live answers are never lip-synchronised.

## Files

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `poster.jpg` | The rest frame R (below), shown until a video plays and with reduced motion. |
| `idle-a.mp4`, `idle-b.mp4`, `idle-c.mp4` | About 24 s each: R brought to life (breathing, slight head movement, real blinks). Manifest kind `listen`. |
| `talk-4s.mp4` … `talk-12s.mp4` | Five generic speaking performances (3.8, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0 and 12.0 s) for live answers. Manifest kind `talk`. |
| `answers/{pregnancy,clinic,employer}.mp4` | One performance per prepared answer, timed to its recording; lip-synced when the manifest says so. |
| `speech/{pregnancy,clinic,employer}.mp3` | The three prepared answers, ElevenLabs voice Chris. |
| `presence.json` | Durations, transcripts, voice, lip-sync flag and attribution. |

All videos: full frame scaled to 1280x720, 25 fps, H.264, no audio track. Durations in `presence.json` come only from the files: after replacing any file, run

```sh
node scripts/avatar/sync-desk-manifest.mjs                       # rewrite every duration from ffprobe
node scripts/avatar/sync-desk-manifest.mjs --lip-sync all=musetalk  # after installing lip-synced answers
node scripts/avatar/sync-desk-manifest.mjs --check               # exit 1 if anything is stale or malformed
```

The script also checks size, frame rate and the absence of audio, and warns when the first and last frames of the files differ in mean brightness by more than 0.75 luma levels (they all show R, so a difference flickers at every join). `tests/avatar-presence.test.ts` checks the manifest against the files independently, without ffprobe.

## One rest pose

R is source frame 406 (16.24 s, frame numbers 0-based): hands clasped on the desk, mouth closed, looking into the camera. It is the last calm moment of the clip, after he finishes speaking. Every file begins and ends on R; the poster is R itself (an exact BT.709 conversion of the source's YUV for that frame, JPEG quality 95).

## Idle: the rest frame brought to life

The idle clips do not replay footage. Each is R, re-rendered frame by frame by a small generator with a fixed random seed (21, 23 and 26):

- **Breathing**: irregular cycles of 3.5-4.6 s that lift the chest and shoulders by 0.9-1.4 px (at 1280 wide). Weight maps keep the hands, the desk and the background still.
- **Head movement**: slow drifts of the head and neck, at most 1.8 px sideways, 1.0 px up or down and 0.28° of roll about the neck, with pauses between moves.
- **Blinks**: real blinks from the same clip (eyes closing at source frames 178-181 and 297-300, reopening at 203-208 and 320-325), pasted onto R at irregular intervals; about one in five is a double blink.
- **Grain**: fresh sensor-like noise at the level measured on the source background, so the picture is not frozen.

All motion is zero, with zero velocity, on the first and last frames; no blink falls in the first or last second; the grain ramps in and out over 6 frames. Each clip is encoded from the source's YUV with one keyframe and higher quality on the first and last frames. The idle build measured its first and last frames within 0.1 grey levels of R.

The player plays the three idles in random order, never the same twice in a row, each to its end.

## Speech: real gestures, rising from and settling into R

A speaking file has three parts, all at the source's own speed (no slowing, no reversed speech):

1. **Rise**: R, then the last 0.2-1.3 s of the real settle played in reverse (he leans in and lifts his head, hands still clasped), morphed over 5 frames into the body: source frames 181-182, where the real footage opens the clasped hands (337 in talk-4s). The middle frame of that morph shows half-blended fingers.
2. **Body**: real forward footage from frames 182-351, the part where he talks with his hands (talk-4s uses only 337-406). Longer files jump back or skip forward inside it; each such join is a 4-frame morph aligned with optical flow.
3. **Settle**: forward to frame 406, either the real ending 352-406 or a jump into its last part (373-376), so the last frame is R.

There are only about 7 s of talking footage, so every speaking file reuses the same gestures, and long answers repeat them.

- **Live answers** have no performance of their own. The player covers the audio with generic files at one playback rate between 0.92 and 1.08, choosing the combination that misses the audio least (ending early costs three times as much as running on, and playing the same file twice in a row costs one second), then the fewest files. Every join between them is a return to R. If the audio ends early, the current file plays its settle when that is within 1.5 s of its end; otherwise it dissolves into idle over 340 ms.
- **Prepared answers** each have one file, cut so that its pauses fall in the recording's pauses and its settle ends a few hundred milliseconds after the last word. The page starts the voice only once the file is ready, starts the picture when the audio actually plays, keeps the two within 80 ms and returns to idle when the video ends. A drift of up to 300 ms is closed by playing the picture up to 10 % faster or slower; only a larger jump is a seek, because these files have a single keyframe and a seek near their end decodes the whole file (about 200 ms of frozen picture in Firefox). The picture pauses when the voice buffers, and the voice is held when the picture freezes, but not while the picture finishes a seek of its own.

## Lip-sync

The prepared answers are lip-synced offline with MuseTalk 1.5 on the CPU, through `scripts/avatar/render-musetalk.py` (MuseTalk's MIT notice is in `scripts/avatar/MUSETALK_NOTICE.txt`; the model files keep their own licences, listed in `docs/AVATAR_PERFORMANCE_RESEARCH.md`):

- `--vae tiny` (the TAESD autoencoder, MIT) and `--blend mouth`, which replaces only the mouth region and keeps the original eyes, glasses, stubble and body; 6 threads.
- The face box starts at 554,135-706,313 on R and follows the head every frame. The generated mouth fades in over the first 6 frames and fades out over 6 frames starting 3 frames after the last sound, so the files still start and end on R. Where fingertips reach the chin, the original pixels are kept.
- The generated mouth is composited onto the speaking performance's own frames before its only encode (`lipsync/scripts/composite-final.py`), with the original stubble texture restored outside the lips.
- Cost on this machine: about 3 s per frame and about 4 GB of memory (6.5 to 10.5 minutes per answer).

What it shows at page size: no visible seam, glasses and stubble intact. Weaknesses: the lips seldom close fully (also in pauses), and the mouth is slightly softer than the rest of the face. Lip closures for m, b and p were checked by eye in 9 places: 6 close, "pregnancy" and "baby" in the employer answer do not, and "maternity" in the clinic answer closes only partly. The mouth seems to move about one frame ahead of the audio.

## Voice

The three answers are ElevenLabs text-to-speech in the premade voice **Chris** (voice id `iP95p4xoKVk53GoZ742B`), chosen by the product owner by ear on 15 November 2026. The texts are in `src/data/avatar-performance-dialogue.json` and in `presence.json`; the pregnancy answer was shortened for this voice. Durations: pregnancy 11.47 s, clinic 7.34 s, employer 10.03 s (mono MP3, 44.1 kHz, 128 kbit/s). The ElevenLabs model and settings used were not recorded with the files. No key is stored in the repository.

Live replies are read aloud by `/api/aidaddy/speak` in the server's own ElevenLabs voice (`ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID`, or AIDaddy's designed voice), which is not necessarily Chris.

## Measured limits

- 16.5 s of footage, about 7 s of it talking: gestures repeat within and between answers. There is no footage of desk work, typing or looking away.
- Joins are crossfades between two encodes of R. Every file is encoded from the source's YUV with BT.709 tags; first frames are within 0.02 and last frames within 0.12 grey levels of R, and the mean brightness of all first and last frames spans 0.06 luma levels (`sync-desk-manifest.mjs`).
- The prepared answers are fitted to the Chris recordings and run about 1 s past their audio (pregnancy 12.44 s, clinic 8.32 s, employer 11.12 s); the hands are back on the clasp 0.12-0.19 s after the last sound.
- Known visible defects: the half-blended fingers in the rise (one frame in every speaking file); in pregnancy (source 324 to 220, output frame 125) and talk-12s (326 to 214, output frame 108) the left hand moves about 80 px in one frame with a faint ghost; the generic files show his mouth closed for short stretches (about 1.2 s in total in talk-12s), and a long live answer alternates the same two or three files.
- Checked on 15 November in Firefox (Playwright) at 1440x900 and 390x844: idle joins, cuts between idle and answers, picture-voice offset during the three answers (median under 15 ms, at most about 65 ms while the voice plays), Stop, pause and resume, voice off, and a live answer. Not checked: Chrome or Safari, audible quality.

## Reproducing the videos

The idle, speaking and lip-sync build scripts are not in the repository. They live in the git-ignored work folder `.data/avatar-v2/v3/` of the `vlad/avatar-concept` worktree, with many exploratory scripts beside the ones the build needs:

| Folder | Entry point | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `idle/scripts` | `build.sh` (decode, noise measurement, person matte, weight maps, blink bank, then `render_all.sh`, which runs `gen_idle.py <seed> <out>` three times and `make_poster.py`) | the source clip at `/tmp/avatar-performance-sources/5941016.mp4`, ffmpeg, Python with OpenCV and NumPy |
| `talk/scripts` | `run-all.sh` (decode, analysis, audio pauses, pose matching); its frame cache in `talk/work` is used by the final build | the same, plus the three MP3s for fitting the prepared answers |
| `final/scripts` | `plan_final.py` (writes `final/work/plans.json`), `render_final.py <name>...` (encodes through `fengine.py`), `verify_final.py` (joins on R) | the talk analysis and cache above |
| `lipsync/scripts` | `run-final.sh` (MuseTalk on the three answers, a wrapper around `scripts/avatar/render-musetalk.py`), then `composite-final.py <scene>` | MuseTalk 1.5, Whisper-tiny and TAESD weights in `.data/avatar-performance/muse`, the CPU Torch environment described in `docs/AVATAR_PERFORMANCE_RESEARCH.md`, about 4 GB of free memory |

To rebuild elsewhere, copy those folders, adjust the absolute paths in the shell scripts, run them, copy the outputs into `public/avatar/desk/` under the names above, and run `scripts/avatar/sync-desk-manifest.mjs`.
